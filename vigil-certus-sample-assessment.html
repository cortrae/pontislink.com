CIS Controls v8 IG1 Gap Assessment

A sample readiness review, run in the same format used for a Vigil Certus client engagement — from intake through a prioritized remediation roadmap.

Framework: CIS Controls v8, Implementation Group 1 Subject: Solo-operator SaaS business Stack: Google Workspace + CRM/automation platform

Intake Summary

  • Team size: Solo operator (owner-only access to core systems)
  • Core stack: Google Workspace for email, docs, and identity; a CRM/automation platform for client relationships; a payment processor for recurring billing
  • MFA status: Enabled across email, CRM, and payment processor accounts
  • Written policies: None currently documented
  • Data handled: Client contact information and business data across several product lines; payment card data is processor-hosted, not stored directly

Why This Matters

A business selling recurring services to other companies eventually gets asked a version of "how do you protect our data," whether that's a vendor security questionnaire, a cyber insurance renewal, or a larger client doing basic diligence before signing. Most small operators have never been asked to formalize what they already do, so the gap isn't usually the technology, it's the documentation and the process behind it.

What This Actually Means

Two encouraging findings: MFA is genuinely in place everywhere, the single highest-leverage control and the one most solo operators skip. And the core platforms provide strong baseline encryption and logging by default, so the underlying infrastructure isn't the weak point.

The real gap is documentation and process, not technology. Nothing here requires buying new tools. It requires writing down what already exists, adding backups, and building the two or three processes that matter most before they're urgently needed, specifically before adding a contractor, and before a client or partner asks for proof.

Prioritized Remediation Roadmap

Do first — this month, low effort, highest leverage:

  1. Turn on automated backup/export for CRM data and business documents. This is the largest actual exposure right now, since there is currently no recovery path if access is lost or an account is compromised.
  2. Verify SPF/DKIM/DMARC are correctly configured on the sending domain. Affects email deliverability and closes a phishing/spoofing gap.
  3. Write a one-page software/SaaS inventory: every tool with access to client or business data, what it touches, who has login access.

Do next — before adding any contractor or VA:

  1. Draft a written information security policy covering account access, data handling, and device use.
  2. Draft a one-page incident response plan: what to do first if an account is compromised, who to notify, how to rotate credentials.
  3. Set up endpoint protection on the primary device(s) used to run the business.

Do before it's asked for — positions the business ahead of a client or partner request:

  1. Build a vendor/service provider inventory documenting what each platform does with client data, and where their own compliance posture stands.
  2. Establish a quarterly review cadence: revisit the software inventory, confirm backups are working, confirm MFA is still enforced everywhere.

Evidence Folder Starter

Once the items above are complete, the following should be kept on file and easy to produce on request:

  • Screenshot or export confirming MFA enforcement across all core platforms
  • SPF/DKIM/DMARC verification record
  • Backup configuration confirmation and one successful test restore
  • Signed information security policy and incident response plan
  • Software/vendor inventory, dated and version-controlled
