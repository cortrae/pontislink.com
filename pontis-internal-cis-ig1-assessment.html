What This Actually Means, in Plain Terms

The two encouraging findings: MFA is genuinely in place everywhere, which is the single highest-leverage control and the one most solo operators skip. And the core platforms (Google Workspace, GHL) provide strong baseline encryption and logging by default, so the underlying infrastructure isn't the weak point.

The real gap is documentation and process, not technology. Nothing here requires buying new tools. It requires writing down what already exists, adding backups, and building the two or three processes that matter most before they're urgently needed, specifically before adding a contractor or VA, and before a client or partner asks for proof.

Prioritized Remediation Roadmap

Do first (this month, low effort, highest leverage):

  1. Turn on automated backup/export for GHL data and business documents. This is the single largest actual exposure right now, since there is currently no recovery path if GHL access is lost or an account is compromised.
  2. Verify SPF/DKIM/DMARC are correctly configured on the sending domain. Directly affects email deliverability for cold outreach campaigns and closes a phishing/spoofing gap.
  3. Write a one-page software/SaaS inventory: every tool with access to client or business data, what data it touches, who has login access.

Do next (before adding any contractor or VA):

  1. Draft a written information security policy (even a short one) covering account access, data handling, and device use.
  2. Draft a one-page incident response plan: what to do first if an account is compromised, who to notify, how to rotate credentials.
  3. Set up endpoint protection on the primary device(s) used to run the business.

Do before it's asked for (positions Pontis/Vigil ahead of a client or partner request):

  1. Build a vendor/service provider inventory documenting what Google, GHL, and the payment processor do with client data, and where their own compliance posture stands (SOC 2 status, etc.), since this is exactly the kind of question Vigil Certus clients will eventually be asked and it's worth Pontis modeling the practice.
  2. Establish a quarterly review cadence: revisit the software inventory, confirm backups are working, confirm MFA is still enforced everywhere.

Evidence Folder Starter

Once the items above are complete, the following should be kept on file and easy to produce on request:

  • Screenshot or export confirming MFA enforcement across Google Workspace, GHL, and payment processor
  • SPF/DKIM/DMARC verification record
  • Backup configuration confirmation and one successful test restore
  • Signed information security policy and incident response plan
  • Software/vendor inventory, dated and version-controlled

Note on use as a Dream 100 artifact: This same structure, intake through remediation roadmap, is what gets run for a real prospect: swap the entity details, adjust for actual team size and stack, and the format holds.
